## What the model is

Per the upstream `model.config` this frame is NXP's HoverGames kit (the
FMUK66 flight controller on a carbon quad frame), the airframe PX4's Gazebo
simulation ships as `x500`. It is **not** a mesh of a Holybro X500 V2, and it
carries no NXP or Holybro branding here.

## What was changed

- **The frame mesh is split** into `meshes/parts/<Part>.dae`, one file per
  node of the upstream visual scene (CarbonFiber, Metal, FMUK66, FMURubber,
  LandingFoam, LandingPlastic, LandingRubber, RailsAntennaHolder,
  RailsRubber), each keeping only its own geometry. OmniSim's `Mesh` node
  cannot select a sub-mesh, so a part needs its own file to carry its own
  material. The geometry is upstream's, except the two heaviest parts:
  `FMUK66` (47,424 triangles) and `LandingPlastic` (60,436) are simplified
  to 30,000 faces each and stored as `.obj` (quadric decimation,
  `fast_simplification`; bounds unchanged), because the renderer warns above
  100,000 vertices. Both are untextured flat-colour parts.
- **Materials are OmniSim's**: `CF.png` on the carbon parts, metallic
  metal, flat colours elsewhere (URDF `<texture>` and `<omnisim>` material
  extensions, imported since 2026-09-25).
- **Rotor blur discs** (`x500_prop_disc`, alpha 0) are added to each
  propeller link; `mavic_omnilink_bridge` fades them in with rotor speed.
- **Joint and sensor names are the Mavic's** (`front left propeller`, …,
  the camera gimbal chain, `inertial unit`, `gps`) so the same bridge flies
  it. The gimbal is copied from `projects/robots/dji/mavic/urdf/` and placed
  under the frame's nose; the upstream model has no gimbal.
- **Propeller and gimbal links carry no collision** — the frame's five boxes
  (plate, two legs, two skids) do; worlds need
  `WorldInfo.newtonCompoundColliders TRUE`, or only the first box survives
  and the skids sink into the floor.

## Flight model (declared by the world, not by this package)

The URDF describes the airframe; how it flies is declared by the world in
the robot's `customData` (`rotor_dynamics`, read by
`mavic_omnilink_bridge` / `mavic_dynamics.py`). The showcase world
[`x500_arena.omniworld`](../../../samples/demos/worlds/showcase/x500_arena.omniworld)
uses:

| Term | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `k_thrust` | 0.0011073 | whole-robot mass 2.1186 kg: m g / (4 · 68.5²), 68.5 rad/s being the bridge's hover speed |
| `k_torque` | 1.0663e-05 | the Mavic's torque/thrust ratio at this `k_thrust` |
| rotor anchors | ±0.174 m, centred on the whole-robot CoM (+2.63 mm x, +0.15 mm y) | centred on the link origin instead, it crept 0.35 m forward on every landing |
| `motor_tau_s` | 0.04 | rotor spin-up lag |
| ground effect | rotor radius 0.165 m | 13-inch props, scale 0.846 |
| `drag_coeff` | 0.03 N/(m/s)² | ~0.05 m² frontal area, Cd ≈ 1 |
| wind | mean (0.3, 0.1, 0) m/s, gusts σ 0.4 m/s, τ 2 s, seed 7 | **MODELLED**, seeded — not measured air |
| control | `HEADING_HOLD` 1, `XY_I_RADIUS_M` 0.5, `MAX_YAW_DISTURBANCE` 10 | the Mavic gains wandered in heading and overshot by 12 cm |

Measured with those values on `x500_arena.omniworld` (scripted takeoff to
1 m, 4 s hover, 2 m move, land; 1080p capture; machine 9722d23d12a3,
2026-09-25): hover sway 0.8 cm, heading within ±1°, 4.1 cm overshoot on the
2 m move, touchdown at ~0 m/s with 6.6 cm drift. None of it is
validated against a real x500's flight.
